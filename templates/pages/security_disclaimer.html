Security Architecture & Electoral Disclaimer

Distinguishing web application governance controls from mathematical End-to-End Verifiable public election systems.

Important Portfolio Disclosure

BallotBridge is an advanced portfolio application demonstrating modern full-stack web engineering, transactional integrity, and secret-ballot workflow modeling. It is designed for educational, organizational, and simulated governance contexts.

Why Web-Application Controls Differ from Public Electoral Infrastructure

In public statutory elections (e.g. municipal, state, or federal parliamentary elections), standard web application architectures (such as relational databases, HTTPS sessions, and server-side authentication) are insufficient to meet international electoral standards. Key reasons include:

1. End-to-End Verifiability (E2EV)

A true E2EV system allows any voter to verify that their ballot was cast-as-intended, recorded-as-cast, and tallied-as-recorded without trusting the server administrators or the operating system. This is achieved via homomorphic encryption (e.g. ElGamal) or zero-knowledge cryptographic proofs rather than database rows.

2. Coercion Resistance & Receipt-Freeness

In real remote voting, voters may be coerced by family members, employers, or criminal syndicates. Protocols such as Juels-Catalano-Jakobsson (JCJ) or Civitas allow voters to cast fake ballots or overwrite coerced votes using secret credentials. In web simulations, receipt tokens only prove turnout, not candidate choices.

3. Hardware Security Modules (HSM) & Client Compromise

Standard web browsers are susceptible to local spyware, keyloggers, and malware that could alter a voter's selection before transmission. Real-world electronic voting requires voter-verifiable paper audit trails (VVPAT) or audited dedicated client isolation.

What BallotBridge Implements

Within the context of a modern web application, BallotBridge implements the highest attainable application-level governance safeguards:

  • Database-level separation of anonymous ballots and voter participation ledgers.
  • Atomic database transactions preventing race conditions and double voting.
  • Server-side validation on every voting endpoint, regardless of frontend state.
  • Tamper-evident audit logging of system and administrative events.
  • Sealed tally counts until authorized publication after closure.
